# Case Study: Identity Governance Framework for Non-Human Identities in a Hybrid Multi-Cloud Environment

## Context

A large financial services institution operating a hybrid multi-cloud environment (Azure, GCP, IBM) required a governance framework to manage the growing population of Non-Human Identities (NHI) — service accounts, machine identities, API credentials, and workload identities — alongside a parallel effort to establish governance standards for Agentic AI identities entering the environment. No consistent cross-platform policy existed for how these identities were provisioned, certified, or retired, creating regulatory and operational exposure.

## Scope & Role

- Design and implementation of an Identity Governance (IGA) framework covering identity lifecycle, least-privilege enforcement, and access certification across three cloud platforms.
- Definition of GRC and regulatory compliance policy specific to NHI categories, aligned with Zero Trust principles.
- Establishment of governance standards for a new identity category (AI agent identities) with no precedent internally — covering access control, lifecycle management, and risk posture.
- Authorship of internal normative documentation used as the policy reference across engineering, security, and compliance functions.
- Delivery coordinated within an Agile/Scrum operating model — backlog grooming, sprint planning, and OKR tracking — to sequence policy rollout against platform and engineering dependencies.

## Approach

1. **Discovery & requirements gathering** — mapped existing identity types and ownership gaps across the three cloud platforms through structured sessions with platform engineering, security, and risk/compliance stakeholders.
2. **Framework design** — translated regulatory and security requirements (Zero Trust, least privilege) into a normative policy structure that could be applied consistently regardless of underlying platform.
3. **Dependency management** — sequenced rollout to account for platform-specific constraints (Azure, GCP, IBM), coordinating with engineering teams on implementation timing.
4. **Operationalization** — embedded the resulting policies into recurring governance activities (access certification cycles, identity lifecycle reviews) rather than treating them as one-time deliverables.
5. **Cross-functional delivery cadence** — used daily stand-ups, sprint planning, and OKR definition to keep the governance workstream visible and prioritized alongside engineering delivery.

## Design Targets

Framed as governance design scope rather than measured outcomes:

- Full-coverage policy applicability across all three cloud platforms in use (no platform exceptions).
- Defined access certification cadence for all NHI categories, replacing ad hoc or undocumented review practices.
- A governance model for AI agent identities extensible as the agent population grows, rather than scoped to current volume only.
- Policy documentation structured to be audit-ready against standard regulatory compliance and risk management expectations.
